A — Illuminance fundamentals

Photometry
A.1
Illuminance E
E · lux (lx) · lm / m²

Luminous flux received per unit area of a surface. Measures how much light falls on a plane — independent of surface colour or reflectance. 1 lux = 1 lumen per square metre.

Feel it: full sun ~100 000 lx · overcast day ~10 000 lx · office 300–500 lx · corridor 100 lx · candlelit 10 lx
SI quantity · IEC 60050-845
A.2
Luminous flux Φ
Φ · lumen (lm)

Total light power emitted by a source, weighted by the eye's spectral sensitivity (photopic curve). This is the figure on the lamp packaging — output before any fitting or room losses.

Typical outputs: E27 bulb 800 lm · GU10 spot 350 lm · 600×600 LED panel 3 000–5 000 lm · high-bay 15 000–30 000 lm
SI quantity · CIE 015:2018 · photopic V(λ) curve
A.3
Luminous intensity I
I · candela (cd) · lm / sr

Flux per unit solid angle in a given direction. Describes how a fitting concentrates light — identical flux can produce a narrow intense beam or a soft diffuse glow depending on I(β).

Distribution shapes: omni I₀ = Φ/4π · Lambertian panel I₀cosβ, I₀ = Φ/π · spot I₀cosⁿβ, I₀ = Φ(n+1)/2π
SI base unit · IES TM-25 · EULUMDAT format
A.4
Luminance L
L · cd / m²

What the eye perceives: flux per unit area leaving a surface toward the viewer. Luminance drives glare — a bright bare lamp in a dark room causes eye-strain even at low illuminance levels.

Glare rule: limit luminaire surface luminance to <1 000 cd/m² above 65° from nadir for office UGR ≤ 19 (EN 12464-1 Annex A)
SI quantity · CIBSE Guide A §2 · EN 12464-1:2021 §5.4
A.5
Maintained illuminance Ēm
Ēm · lx · time-averaged minimum

The minimum acceptable average illuminance over the reference plane at the end of the maintenance cycle, after lamp ageing and dust accumulation. All EN 12464-1 targets are maintained values.

Installed = Ēm ÷ MF — a 400 lx maintained target with MF 0.80 requires a fresh-lamp average of 500 lx at install
EN 12464-1:2021 §5.3.1 · CIE S 008
A.6
Maintenance factor MF
MF · 0–1 · dimensionless

Ratio of illuminance at end-of-period to initial. Combines lamp lumen depreciation (LLMF), luminaire dirt depreciation (LDD) and room surface dirt depreciation (RSDD).

Typical: clean office LED 0.85 · industrial LED 0.75 · dusty factory 0.65 · outdoor 0.50–0.60
EN 12464-1:2021 §5.3.2 · CIBSE LG7

B — Quality & comfort

Perception
B.1
Uniformity ratio U₀
U₀ = Emin / Eavg

Lowest-to-average illuminance ratio on the reference plane. Low uniformity creates harsh pools and dark zones that force the eye to adapt repeatedly, causing fatigue.

Targets: EN 12464-1 task area ≥ 0.6 · IS 3646 / PPS ≥ 0.8 · circulation ≥ 0.4 · sport ≥ 0.7
EN 12464-1:2021 §5.5 · IS 3646:2015 §6
B.2
Unified Glare Rating UGR
UGR · scale 10–30 · lower = better

Standardised measure of discomfort glare. Computed from luminance in the glare zone, background luminance and solid angle. Not shown by the tool — assessed separately from the photometric file.

Limits by task: precision drawing ≤ 16 · office ≤ 19 · retail ≤ 22 · rough factory ≤ 28
EN 12464-1:2021 Annex A · CIE 117
B.3
Colour rendering index Ra
Ra (CRI) · 0–100 · higher = better

How accurately a source renders object colours versus a reference illuminant. Critical for retail, food, healthcare and anywhere colour decisions are made under the installed light.

Minimums: general ≥ 80 · retail / grooming ≥ 90 · museum / colour-matching ≥ 95 · surgery ≥ 90 + R9 ≥ 50
EN 12464-1:2021 §5.6 · CIE 13.3
B.4
Correlated colour temperature CCT
CCT · Kelvin (K)

Colour appearance of white light from warm (orange) to cool (blue). Not a quality metric — higher K is not better. CCT should match the ambient mood and task: warm for residential, neutral-cool for task work.

Bands: warm white <3000K · neutral 3000–4500K · cool >4500K · daylight 5500–6500K
CIE 15:2018 · Kruithof heuristic (non-normative)
B.5
Inverse-square law
E = I · cosθ / d²

Illuminance from a point source falls as the square of distance — double the distance, quarter the light. The cosθ receiver-tilt penalty halves illuminance on a surface angled 60° from the source.

Ceiling height impact: raising from 2.7 m to 4 m roughly halves work-plane illuminance from identical fixtures (d² factor)
Radiometry first principles · IES Handbook 10th ed.
B.6
Luminous efficacy η
η = Φ / P · lm / W

Lumens delivered per watt consumed. Governs energy use. LED technology has pushed efficacy from ~15 lm/W (incandescent) to 80–200 lm/W for modern modules. System luminaire efficacy (SLE) is always lower than lamp efficacy due to optical losses.

Typical SLE: LED downlight 60–90 · LED panel 80–120 · high-bay 100–160 lm/W
IEC 62612 · EU Reg. 2019/2020 (ErP)

C — The lumen method

Design calculation
C.1
Lumen method — the quick design check
Ēm = (N · Φ · UF · MF) / A
N = luminaire count  ·  Φ = lm per luminaire  ·  UF = utilisation factor  ·  MF = maintenance factor  ·  A = floor area m²
N = Ēm · A / (Φ · UF · MF)   (rearranged to find required count)

The lumen method includes interreflection from walls and ceiling via UF. The point-by-point tool below deliberately omits interreflection — real rooms with light surfaces read 10–30% higher than the tool's direct-only value. The lumen-method line in the tool panel is the physical upper bound.

CIBSE Code for Lighting §3 · EN 12464-1:2021 Annex B
C.2
Utilisation factor UF
UF · 0.30–0.85 · dimensionless

Fraction of total emitted flux that reaches the reference plane. Depends on room cavity ratio, luminaire distribution and surface reflectances (ceiling typically 70%, walls 50%, floor 20%). Looked up from the manufacturer's UF table.

Typical: small dark room 0.35–0.45 · medium office 0.50–0.60 · large open-plan light surfaces 0.60–0.75
CIBSE Code for Lighting §3.4 · EN 13032-4
C.3
Room cavity ratio RCR
RCR = 5 · Hm · (W + D) / (W · D)

Single number describing room geometry as seen by a downlighter. Hm = cavity height (ceiling to work-plane), W = width, D = depth. High RCR (narrow, tall room) means more light hits walls → UF falls. Used to enter the UF table.

Example: 10×8 m, H=3 m, WP=0.8 → Hm=2.2, RCR = 5×2.2×18/80 = 2.5 (medium)
CIBSE Code for Lighting §3.4 · IES HB-0-00

E — Reading the tool output

Interpretation
Average (Ēm)
247 lx

Mean illuminance across the work plane. Compare directly against the maintained target. EN 12464-1 specifies this — not the max, not the centre-point.

Minimum (Emin)
98 lx

Darkest point on the grid. Used to compute U₀. Consistently low at the perimeter means fixtures are spaced too far from the wall or each other.

Uniformity U₀
0.40

Min ÷ avg. Below 0.6 is visually patchy — users can see bright pools and dark gaps. Reduce fixture spacing or add rows/columns until U₀ ≥ 0.6.

Area ≥ target %
62%

Fraction of the work plane meeting the maintained target. A well-designed scheme targets ≥ 90% — perimeter dead zones are acceptable if the task area is covered.

Lumen-method estimate
~310 lx

Whole-room average including interreflection. With bounce enabled the heatmap is anchored to this figure; the gap between it and the direct average is the indirect component, shown beneath the UF slider.

Pass / Fail badges
Avg · U₀

Green on both = design meets standard. U₀ fail with Avg pass = distribution problem, not total flux — add fixtures or reduce spacing.

F — Placement heuristics

Rules of thumb
F.1

The 1.5H spacing rule

Maximum centre-to-centre fixture spacing = 1.5 × mounting height (ceiling to work-plane). Exceeding this almost always fails U₀ ≥ 0.6 for a regular grid.

Smax = 1.5 × Hm
F.2

Wall offset — half spacing

First fixture row should be no more than 0.5× the inter-fixture spacing from the perimeter wall. Use 0.3× for rooms where tasks occur near walls (kitchens, workbenches).

wall offset ≤ 0.5 × S
F.3

The 3:1 accent ratio

Feature or display lighting should be 3–10× the ambient level. Below 3× it reads flat; above 15× it forces uncomfortable visual adaptation.

Eaccent = 3–10 × Eambient
F.4

CCT paired to illuminance

The Kruithof heuristic (non-normative): dim rooms feel natural at warm CCT; bright rooms at higher CCT. Bright + warm reads yellow; dim + cool reads clinical.

<300 lx → 2700–3000K  ·  ≥300 lx → 3000–4000K+
F.5

Design above maintained target

Always over-design by the MF. A 400 lx maintained target at MF 0.80 needs 500 lx at install — otherwise the room is already below target on the first day.

Einitial = Ēm ÷ MF
F.6

Vertical-plane check for social spaces

The work-plane calculation covers horizontal surfaces only. For hospitality, also check vertical illuminance on faces at 1.2 m height — aim for 50–150 lx for comfortable social perception.

Ev at 1.2 m ≥ 50 lx (CIBSE LG2)
