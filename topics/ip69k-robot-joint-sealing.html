01 — Acceptance conditions

Close range, hot, and rotating under the nozzle

IP69K originates in ISO 20653 (automotive/DIN 40050-9), not IEC 60529 directly, though it's now widely cross-referenced from it. It pairs the standard dust-tight test with a water test that is nothing like the others in this family: short range, high pressure, hot, and applied from four fixed angles while the sample rotates. This is the rating a food-grade or pharma cobot joint has to clear.

Fig. 1 — Every point on the enclosure gets all four spray angles as the table rotates, at a range close enough that the jet is still nearly full-strength on arrival.

02 — The variable the other ratings don't have

80 °C changes the elastomer, not just the pressure

Every other sheet in this family treats temperature as a secondary effect — the thermal-cooldown vent problem in IP67 Sec. 02. IP69K makes 80 °C water part of the acceptance test itself, and that number, not the 8–10 MPa pressure, is what actually eliminates most elastomer choices.

NBR is out. Standard nitrile's usable ceiling (per the IP67 elastomer table) sits right at or below the test temperature, repeated daily, for years. This is the one rating in the family where the default IP67 compound choice is actively wrong rather than merely conservative.

FKM or EPDM, chosen by chemistry, not just heat range. Both tolerate 80 °C water. EPDM is the usual pick for a caustic clean-in-place protocol; FKM if the process also exposes the joint to oils or solvents EPDM would swell in. Silicone (VMQ) is not a candidate here at all — it's a static-only compound and this application is dynamic at the shaft.

Thermal expansion changes squeeze mid-test. An elastomer's coefficient of thermal expansion is roughly an order of magnitude higher than the metal gland around it. A gland set for 25 °C nominal squeeze runs measurably tighter once the part itself reaches 80 °C — usually a benefit for sealing, but it consumes free volume, so groove fill (IP67 Sec. 04) must be checked hot, not just at MMC/LMC at room temperature.

Thermal shock is part of the real duty cycle. A cold joint hit with 80 °C water, repeatedly, is a thermal-shock load on any bonded or coated component — conformal coating, potted connector back-ends, adhesive-bonded seal washers. Qualify those bonds through the thermal cycle, not just the pressure test.

03 — Detail design

What the close range and the pressure change

Add, versus IP67
  • FKM or EPDM elastomer selected for the CIP chemistry
  • Squeeze/fill re-checked at 80 °C, not just at ambient
  • Vent membrane rated for the working pressure, shrouded from direct spray
  • Thermal-shock qualification on all bonded/potted joints
  • Corrosion-resistant fasteners and housing finish for daily caustic exposure
Still true from IP67 — unchanged
  • O-ring gland squeeze/fill targets and tolerance-stack method
  • Bonded washers or blind holes on every fastener
  • Counterface finish and lead-free grinding on the rotary seal
  • Cable jacket wicking risk (IP67 Fig. 7) — if anything, worse at 100 mm range

04 — The requirement the IP code doesn't cover

Passing the spray test isn't the same as passing hygienic design

Food and pharma deployments layer a second, unrelated requirement on top of IP69K: the enclosure has to be cleanable, not just sealed. IEC 60529 and ISO 20653 are silent on this — it comes from EHEDG or 3-A sanitary design guidance instead, and a joint can pass IP69K while failing it outright.

  1. No horizontal ledgesAnywhere product residue can poolEvery flat-on-top feature — a boss, a flange lip, a cable gland shoulder — is a place biofilm accumulates between a wash cycle that only tests ingress, not residue. Slope or radius every surface that faces up.
  2. Continuous, cleanable welds and sealsNo crevices under 0.1 mm hidden ones are fineA bolted joint with a gasket that has any exposed crevice at the parting line is a harbourage point regardless of its IP rating. Sanitary designs favour continuous welds or fully-enclosed gaskets with no exposed edge.
  3. Self-draining, not just sealedStanding wash water is a contamination risk on its ownMount so every external surface sheds to a drain point; a feature that holds even a few millilitres after wash-down fails hygienic review independent of whether water got inside the housing.

05 — Verification

Decay testing needs a hot rig, or a documented correlation

The pressure-decay method from the IP67 sheet still applies as the production screen, with one addition: because squeeze changes with temperature (Sec. 02), a decay limit correlated only at ambient can pass a joint that would fail once it actually runs hot. Either run decay screening on a heated fixture, or establish and document the ambient-to-80 °C correlation with calibrated-leak masters before trusting a room-temperature production test alone.
