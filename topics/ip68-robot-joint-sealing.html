01 — What "IP68" legally means

IEC 60529 does not define an IP68 test

Every other rating on this atlas names a fixed apparatus, medium and duration. IPX8 does not. The standard states only that the conditions must be agreed between manufacturer and user and must be more severe than IPX7 — deeper than 1 m, longer than 30 min, or both. An "IP68" claim with no stated depth and duration is not a rating; it's a marketing word.

Always ask: IP68 at what? "IP68" on its own tells you less than "IP67" does. Treat any IP68 claim — yours or a supplier's — as incomplete until it carries a depth and a duration, e.g. IP68 @ 2 m / 24 h. That pair is what actually gets tested, quoted, and defended.

02 — The physics

Depth is a design load, not a marketing tier

The IP67 pressure chart puts 1 m of head at 9.8 kPa — trivial for any seal. That stops being true as declared depth grows, and IP68 is exactly the rating where it starts to matter.

03 — Detail design

What genuinely changes versus IP67

Sustained pressure, not a 30-minute pulse. Elastomer creeps under long-duration load. A squeeze that holds its 20–30% for a half-hour immersion test can relax further over a 24-hour or continuous declared duration. Re-run the compression-set figures from the IP67 gland table at the declared duration, not the 70-hour bench figure alone.

The vent becomes the weak point, or has to become unnecessary. A standard hydrophobic membrane has a water-entry pressure rating — commonly in the tens of kPa. Past a few metres of declared depth, either specify a membrane rated above the working pressure, or design the cavity to not need one (e.g. potted, or genuinely non-hermetic by design, accepting the trade from the IP54 sheet's Sec. 04 in reverse).

Housing stiffness joins the sealing problem. At tens of kPa sustained, a thin-walled cover can deflect enough to reduce gland squeeze at its centre even while the bolted perimeter holds nominal squeeze — a failure mode that a static bench check at zero pressure will not reveal. FEA the cover under the declared pressure, not just the gland cross-section in isolation.

Buoyancy and structural load, if it's an ROV or AGV joint. A robot joint declared IP68 is often on a submersible or wading platform, where the housing carries real hydrostatic structural load in addition to the sealing load — that is a pressure-vessel design problem the IP rating alone does not capture.

04 — Deployment

Where 68 actually shows up on a robot

  1. Submersible / ROV jointsGenuine sustained depthThe one case where IP68 means what it says at face value — declared depth is the operating depth plus margin, and the whole housing is a pressure vessel first, an enclosure second.
  2. Wading AGVs and floor-washing robotsShallow but continuousA mobile base that regularly crosses standing water or operates through a wash cycle longer than 30 minutes needs IP68 at a modest declared depth (often 0.5–1.5 m) rather than IP67, purely on duration.
  3. Marketing headroomThe declaration nobody wrote downThe most common real-world case: a supplier states "IP68" with no depth or duration because the design happens to also pass a longer or deeper informal test than IPX7 required. Treat this as an undocumented IP67 until a declaration exists — see Sec. 05.

05 — Specification

Writing a declaration that means something

  1. State depth and duration together, always"IP68" alone is not a spec line. "IP68 @ 2 m / 2 h" is. Put both numbers in the datasheet, the drawing, and the qualification report.
  2. Declare the water conditionFresh or salt water changes both the density (pressure) and the corrosion exposure. State which was tested, and derate or re-test if the deployment differs.
  3. Confirm it exceeds your own IPX7 result on the same sample typeThe standard's only hard requirement — more severe than IPX7 in depth, duration, or both. Document the comparison explicitly in the test report.
  4. Test at the worst-case tolerance stack, not nominalSame rule as IP67 Sec. 09 — minimum squeeze, maximum groove fill, run at the declared pressure for the declared duration.
  5. Re-verify after any endurance runSustained-pressure creep is cumulative. A unit that passed the declaration new should be re-tested after its thermal and mechanical endurance cycle, not assumed to still hold.
