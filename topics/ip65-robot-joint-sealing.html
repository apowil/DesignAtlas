01 — Acceptance conditions

Dust-tight, plus a jet instead of a dunk

IP65 pairs the same absolute dust-tight test IP67 uses (Cl. 13.4, criterion 6X) with a directed low-pressure jet in place of immersion. It is the rating most factory-floor robots are actually specified to, because most cells see a hose, not a puddle. See the IP67 sheet for the shared joint anatomy and the static-gland rules, which apply unchanged here.

A jet is not "less water" than immersion 9.8 kPa of hydrostatic head from a metre of water is a gentle, sustained, omnidirectional load. A 6.3 mm jet at ≈30 kPa is a violent, momentary, single-direction load that can find a seal lip's least favourable angle and lift it locally — even though the immersion test runs for 30 minutes against the jet's 3. Do not read IP65 as "IP67 minus a bit." It stresses the seal differently, not less.

02 — The governing physics

Dynamic load beats a lip; static load beats a gland

The IP67 pressure chart already ranks these on a log scale — a 5-jet sits at roughly 3× the immersion test's pressure. What matters for design is less the number and more where each type of load acts.

Immersion loads the whole boundary evenly and slowly. Every static gland and the rotary lip see the same 9.8 kPa for the same 30 minutes, from a direction the design can predict — outward-in, uniformly. This is what the squeeze/fill rules in the IP67 gland section are calibrated against.

A jet loads one small area, hard, briefly, from an angle you may not have drawn. The test sweeps the nozzle over the whole enclosure, so every seam and shaft entry gets its turn under the full jet pressure, from whatever angle the sweep happens to hit it at — including glancing angles that can work water sideways into a lip's dust side before it ever builds a differential across the lip itself.

Static glands rarely notice the difference. A correctly squeezed O-ring in a machined gland does not care whether 30 kPa arrives as a jet or 9.8 kPa arrives as a static head — both are trivial next to the seal's actual capability. The gland rules do not change between IP65 and IP67.

The rotary lip is where the two ratings diverge. IP65 does not require the lip to resist a sustained inward differential — there is no 30-minute soak, and (see Sec. 04) the thermal-cooldown vacuum problem is a function of dust-tightness, not water rating, so it is present at both. What IP65 removes is the requirement to survive standing water at the shaft after the test ends. A single well-specified lip, per the IP67 counterface rules, is normally sufficient — the counterface spec does not relax.

03 — Detail design

What actually changes from the IP67 joint

Less than intuition suggests. Because the first numeral is identical, the static-gland and dust-exclusion design is unchanged. The water-side design loosens only where the immersion-specific requirements were.

Unchanged from IP67
  • O-ring gland — squeeze 20–30%, fill 60–85%
  • Counterface finish — Ra 0.2–0.5 µm, plunge-ground
  • Bonded washers or blind holes on every fastener
  • Pressure-equalisation vent — see Sec. 04
  • Sealed connectors, rated only when mated
Loosens for IP65
  • No requirement to survive standing water post-test
  • Dust lip may run lighter duty — less soak time
  • No 1000 mm depth clearance to plan mounting around
  • Cable jacket wicking (IP67 Fig. 7) is a smaller exposure

04 — The part people assume they can skip

You still need the vent

This is the one place engineers most often mis-scope an IP65 design, because the equalisation problem looks like it belongs to the water rating. It doesn't.

Vent requirement tracks the first numeral, not the second A cavity is hermetic — or it isn't — based on whether it is dust-tight (6X). Once it's sealed well enough to stop 75 µm talc, it is sealed well enough to build the same ~12 kPa thermal-cooldown depression the IP67 sheet describes, regardless of whether the water rating is 65, 66, 67 or 69K. That depression exceeds even the 5-jet's ≈30 kPa margin over time, and it acts on every seal in the joint, all day, every duty cycle. Omit the membrane vent because "this one's only 65, not 67," and you have built exactly the failure mode Sec. 02 of the IP67 sheet documents — it will pass qualification cold and leak in the field once it has run hot.

05 — Decision

When 65 is enough, and when it isn't

65 is enough when: the cell is cleaned with a hose or spray wand at typical mains pressure, the joint never sits in standing water through its full range of motion, and there's no requirement to survive a wash-down that outlasts a coffee break.

Step up when: cleaning uses a pressure washer (→ 66), the base or a low link can be submerged by a spill or a floor drain backing up (→ 67), or the process is food, pharma or otherwise requires a documented clean-in-place protocol at temperature (→ 69K).
